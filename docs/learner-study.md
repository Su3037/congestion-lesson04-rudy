# Learner study

**Status: not run.** It needs 3–5 real learners, which only the course author can recruit.

## Protocol (about 40 minutes per learner)

1. Pre-test: the 6-item concept check in section 07 of the lesson page, answered before reading. Each item targets one misconception. Record the first answer to each item.
2. The learner works through the lesson page, one film of their choice and the lab on their own. Do not help.
3. Think-aloud (one learner is enough): ask them to say what they expect before pressing "Add nets one by one" in the lab, then to explain the spread-versus-cluster table (section 03.5) in their own words. Note where they stall.
4. Post-test: the same 6 items, then challenges c4 and c6 in the lab.
5. Item-level results go in the table below, small n and all.

## Items and the misconception each targets

| Item | Misconception | Correct choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| q1 | RUDY says how long the wires are | where the wires will crowd |
| q2 | a pin on a tile edge belongs to the left tile, or to both | column 1 (round down) |
| q3 | the share is HPWL ÷ the box area, or HPWL itself | 11/6 |
| q4 | total demand is unrelated to wirelength | the sum of HPWL |
| q5 | capacity changes demand | demand is unchanged |
| q6 | a smaller total overflow means a better layout on every count | the metric must be named; the cluster's worst tile is worse |
